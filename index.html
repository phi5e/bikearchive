manifesto

    Astalla Bici is like an on-again, off-again relationship. We keep coming back, seemingly forgetting the grind and pain of inserting your rod into places you shouldn’t, using tools that are just wrong for the job, instead focusing on that dopamine hit that may never come, before the inevitable dive into desperation again. The sweet smell of failure, the soothing voice of hope, yet another frame that beckons around the corner: rare, rusty, regal. Beautiful, impossible to pass up.

    We are a DIY bike garage in Munich, Germany. We build what we like, never for money or for clients. We haul those frames home. We build them up from the bones of bikes from the beyond. As we drive the screws in, we drive out the ghost of our better judgement. We lose ourselves in cold steel, and, like glistening, soapy seals, we glide half-naked across the oily garage floor in the everlasting quest to find the Allen Key Size 5. It’s gone again, for fuck’s sake.

    But a garage is just a garage. Parts pile up, unfinished builds hang from the ceiling, chipped-off paint telling of untold hardship it unselfishly bore in its life: they take your breath away – and the room to work. Nobody should keep fifteen bikes, and bikes need to be free anyways, like wild mustangs, or some shit. So some are somberly sold, a few keenly kept. We cut our unkempt hair, air out our sweaty, oily shirts and swear that’s the end of it. Then the next one calls, and we come back together in an endless maelstrom of clanking steel, sweet swears, tough love and moldy rubber. It’s a cycle, after all.
